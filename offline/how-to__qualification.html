how-to/qualification

Bring-up and acceptance matrix

Before the populated board is powered

Ask the assembler or bench-service provider to check component identity, numeric pin mapping, solder joints, shorts, module orientation, power/header continuity and the exact approved module revision. Confirm F1 and USB source current path. Use a current-limited 5 V source and keep the display backlight disabled initially. Rework belongs to that provider, not the home workstation.

Power tests first establish analog/digital rails, 3.0 V reference, startup/off decay, USB current and absence of back-power. Measure current separately with no display, dark display and bright display. Obtain board/module thermal images or measured hot-spot temperatures under worst intended load.

Channel tests

Use a verified low-voltage signal fixture with the same ground and appropriate current limits. Begin with inputs shorted to ground, then step through measured ±1, ±3, ±5, ±8 and ±12 V. Record raw codes and DMM readings for all ten channels. Verify expected open-input bias rather than treating it as a defect or auto-zeroing it away. Test all TIME endpoints/direction and all three RANGE positions.

After normal-range DC validation, check 1 Hz, 10 Hz, 100 Hz, 500 Hz and 1 kHz signals, then deliberately characterize aliasing above the intended range. Compare displayed amplitude against reference instrumentation and the predicted filter transfer function. Distinguish source distortion from this monitor's filtering, conversion and display effects.

Worst-case mux test: alternate a near-positive-full-scale channel and a near-negative-full-scale channel, hold neighboring channels at zero, and measure residual settling/crosstalk with the LCD off and active. Increase rate only while recording discarded/kept conversion timing and overrun counts. An 8 µs slot is acceptable only when its measured error meets the reviewed target.

Separate abnormal-voltage qualification

Do not apply ±24 V merely because the design spreadsheet contains it. First review all clamp paths and absolute maximum constraints, then conduct a controlled powered/unpowered current-limited test at a suitably equipped provider. Inspect both source and internal node currents and any back-powered rail. ESD immunity is a separate test; successful DC injection is not an IEC ESD claim.
