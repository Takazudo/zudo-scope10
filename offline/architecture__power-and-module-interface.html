Power and module interface — stop gate

One source only

Pico H's USB input supplies VBUS_USB. Carrier F1 passes it to +5V_FUSED for the display and the analog regulator. There is no external second supply connection. An eventual suitable USB 5 V source and the Pico USB/VBUS path must satisfy the measured total load and inrush. A 1 A-rated charger is a planned source, not permission to draw 1 A through an unverified PCB/header path.

The analog LDO is TLV75533PDBVR: 3.3 V output, input supply constrained to the manufacturer limits. Use the exact DBV pin assignment (1 IN, 2 GND, 3 EN, 4 NC, 5 OUT). EN is tied to fused 5 V. Use effective input/output capacitance of at least 1 µF as required by the datasheet. No PD voltage negotiation is present.

REF3330 generates 3.0 V and U6D buffers it. The ADC still uses Pico's own reference arrangement; two-point calibration is required to relate the separately generated analog bias to the ADC. The 3.0 V node is not incorrectly connected to Pico ADC_VREF.

USB source contract

The display and analog-rail load on +5V_FUSED is unconditional: it is not gated by USB configuration state, suspend, or any firmware action (F1 passes +5V_FUSED to the display header and the analog regulator unconditionally). Because that load is not enumeration-gated, this module cannot rely on an ordinary host port's staged USB power budget (100 mA before configuration, 500 mA after). design/power-contract.json records the explicit supported-source contract instead: a USB port or supply that delivers at least 500 mA at 5 V from attach, independent of USB enumeration/configuration. Presence of 5 V on VBUS (e.g. a GPIO VBUS-sense pin) does not establish this -- it proves only that a 5 V source is attached, not that it can sustain the required current or that any USB configuration has occurred. An earlier draft's GPIO24/VBUS-sense suggestion is removed for exactly that reason (design/power-contract.json's decision_notes.rejected_gpio24_vbus_sense).

Before configuration, during suspend, across a reset, and while the RP2040 bootrom is in BOOTSEL mode, this module draws more than the USB 2.0 unconfigured/suspend limits on an ordinary host port. Only a source meeting the contract above is supported in any of those states. The RP2040 bootrom presents its own USB descriptor during BOOTSEL, including its own declared max-power value; that value comes from the bootrom, not this project's firmware, and no number is cited for it here pending a bench descriptor dump. A configuration-gated hardware load switch (using real USB configured state, not VBUS presence) is recorded as a future compliant-host option, with no part or firmware mechanism chosen.

Physical header numbering

J20/J30 are the left 20-contact strips: carrier contact k corresponds to Pico position k (1–20). J21/J31 are the right strips: contact k corresponds to Pico position 41-k (40 down to 21). This convention is recorded explicitly in design/circuit.json and checked in the data tests.

J20/J21 accept Pico H. J30/J31 accept the display module. Do not merge these two 40-position interfaces electrically. Only the documented LCD signals, select signals, ground and intended power path pass through the carrier.

G01 — do not route a presumed power connection

The present netlist proposes display Pico-position 39 (VSYS) fed by fused 5 V, with positions 36 (3V3), 37 (3V3_EN), 35 (ADC_VREF) and 40 (VBUS) isolated. This is conditional and not power-qualified. The module schematic was readable as extracted text, but its power-strapping diagram could not be visually verified in this environment. Verify the exact received module revision, its regulator/strap resistors and required GPIO level supplies before finalizing the header footprint/netlist.

The Waveshare board can have extra memory/clock/power-control nets on otherwise spare Pico positions. GP3, GP5 and GP14 on the display header are not automatically tied to the custom carrier's mux controls or timing output. Trace any additional requirements from the correct manufacturer schematic. Do not energize an unverified arrangement and assume a blank screen is a firmware issue.

The independent source files are listed in catalog/sources.json. scripts/fetch_sources.py --execute downloads available originals locally; retrieval does not establish that they have been reviewed.

All address changes occur while mux disable is high in the diagnostic firmware. The LCD backlight is active-low and default ON: GP13 drives the gate of an open-drain N-channel MOSFET (Q1) on the module's LCD_BL node, so GP13 high = backlight off, GP13 low or undriven = backlight on (see "Backlight interface" below). Chip-selects pull high. Do not assume asynchronous analog and digital 3.3 V startup is harmless: qualify power sequencing and unpowered injection into the mux/ADC paths.

Provisional power allocation

Allocate 300 mA to the screen/backlight module, 80 mA to Pico and 40 mA to carrier analog/control circuitry for planning. These are budget allowances, not measured or manufacturer maximum currents. Their sum is 420 mA; verify USB startup/enumeration limits, F1 derating, local capacitance and connector ratings. At 40 mA the analog LDO's ideal 5-to-3.3 V dissipation is about 68 mW, excluding any abnormal condition. Do not infer a thermal pass from this estimate.

Structured module-power evidence (module VSYS capacitance, backlight topology, and the disputed 180 mA claim) lives in design/evidence/module-power.json, one status (verified-schematic / allowance / unknown / rejected) per claim, consumed by scripts/power_budget.py and this page so they cannot disagree. The module's own VSYS capacitors total a verified 11.2 µF; combined with carrier C30 (1 µF) that is a known 12.2 µF carrier+module nominal subtotal, reported against the informal ≤10 µF inrush guidance -- not a claim of measured inrush compliance, since Pico-side bulk capacitance is still unknown. The backlight is a CAT1 regulator feeding LED-A, with three LED cathode returns to ground through R17-R19; CAT1's identity and output current remain unknown. F1 sits between VBUS_USB and +5V_FUSED and carries only the display+analog branch (340 mA), not the full 420 mA total source current: at a 1.5x sizing margin that is a 510 mA target, 10 mA above the selected 1206L050YR candidate's 500 mA nominal hold rating, with no manufacturer temperature-derating curve retained to confirm its actual derated hold current. A PPTC is not a precision USB current limiter. See reports/power-budget.json's f1_branch_sizing and downstream_capacitance_5v_fused sections for the full figures.

design/power-contract.json also holds the declared USB configuration-descriptor value that scripts/power_budget.py checks the 420 mA allowance against. That declared value is currently 250 mA -- the unmodified Pico SDK 2.1.1 default, not a project override: the pinned SDK source does not guard USBD_MAX_POWER_MA with #ifndef, so a compile-time override does not reliably change the compiled descriptor, and no unverified hand-written descriptor replacement is shipped without a hardware bench to check it against (see power-contract.json's override_investigation). Because 420 mA exceeds that declared 250 mA, reports/power-budget.json's usb_budget.vs_declared_limit correctly reports fail, not a masked pass -- this module currently declares less configured power than its own planning allowance, on top of already requiring a source outside the ordinary staged USB budget (see "USB source contract" above).

Backlight interface (GP13) — open-drain stage, default ON, active-low (#41)

Desk evidence (#3, #19) established that the display module's own R16 (10k, VSYS -> LCD_BL) pulls the backlight-enable/CAT1-EN node toward the module's ~5 V VSYS rail. The former carrier wiring (GP13 through R64 straight to LCD_BL, with R88 100k to GND) left an undriven GP13 near 4.5 V, above the RP2040 GPIO absolute maximum of IOVDD + 0.5 V. Neither "remove R88" (node approaches 5 V) nor "accept an estimated clamp current" (relies on an undocumented clamp) met the pin limits, so the carrier circuit is changed instead of waiting for a measurement.

Design now applied in scripts/make_design.py: GP13 -> R64 (33 Ω) -> net LCD_BL_GATE -> gate of Q1, a generic logic-level N-channel MOSFET (catalog/components.json record nmos-ll, no MPN/LCSC code, exact part at G08). R88 (100k) is repurposed as the gate pull-down. Q1's drain is LCD_BL (J30 position 17, header net unchanged) and its source is GND; the module's R16 stays the only pull-up on LCD_BL. The GPIO pad therefore sees only its own drive or 0 V in every state -- USB 4.75–5.25 V, reset, BOOTSEL, either power-up order and normal operation -- and no protection clamp is assumed anywhere (design/evidence/backlight-interface.md has the per-state voltage/current table and the alternatives weighed). scripts/validate_extra.py asserts that GP13's net has no resistive DC path below 1 MΩ to +5V_FUSED, VBUS_USB or LCD_BL.

Default state and polarity are explicit: backlight ON whenever GP13 is low or undriven (reset, BOOTSEL/ROM, before the firmware hook), backlight OFF when GP13 is high. Firmware follows this inversion (firmware/src/lcd_safe_pins.[ch] drives GP13 high early to darken the panel; scope_display_backlight() drives it low to light it; SCOPE_ENABLE_LCD stays default OFF). design/power-contract.json's power_states carry the backlight state per state. CAT1's identity and EN thresholds remain unknown (design/evidence/module-power.json); the off state relies only on LCD_BL being held near 0 V and the on state on the module's own R16 behaviour.

Still OPEN (G01/G06): the assembler/bench provider checks the actual module revision and population (R16, CAT1) and captures the GP13, gate and LCD_BL waveforms from power-on on a factory-assembled prototype before either gate closes (LOCAL-HANDOFF.md G01 items 2–3, G06 step 1; acceptance row BACKLIGHT_GP13_INTERFACE). pending_g01_changes[id=g01-r88-backlight-default] in design/circuit.json records the status DESIGN_APPLIED_BENCH_CHECK_PENDING.
