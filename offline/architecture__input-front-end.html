architecture/input-front-end

Input circuit and analytical checks

Repeated channel

The same circuit is instantiated ten times. A 1 MΩ input resistor feeds a node connected through 200 kΩ to the buffered 3.0 V reference and through 249 kΩ to ground. A 470 pF C0G capacitor makes the first low-pass pole. A unity buffer, 4.7 kΩ / 10 nF C0G low-pass stage and second unity buffer feed the signal multiplexer.

BAV199 provides a low-leakage dual-diode clamp at the biased node. It is silicon, not a Schottky device. The node is protected primarily by high series resistance and the documented amplifier input current path; a diode to a rail is not by itself proof that all absolute-maximum conditions are satisfied.

Two complete channels use one physical TLV9064IDR. U1–U5 provide the 20 input buffer amplifiers. U6 supplies the three post-mux buffers and the reference buffer. The design data enumerate every physical IC pin, rather than inventing multiple U references for the same package.

Transfer function

Let G = 1/1M + 1/200k + 1/249k. For an ideal low-impedance source:

Vnode = ((Vin / 1M) + (Vref / 200k)) / G
      = 0.099839615 × Vin + 1.497594226 V, with Vref = 3.0 V
Vin   = (Vnode - 1.497594226) / 0.099839615

At −12 V the nominal node is 0.299519 V; at +12 V it is 2.695670 V. Both leave nominal headroom on a 3.3 V analog rail. The 0.1% divider-resistor / 0.15% reference corner enumeration spans 0.294579 V at the negative end to 2.700079 V at the positive end. This excludes op-amp offset, temperature, diode leakage, source resistance, ADC errors, capacitance tolerance and PCB parasitics. It is not a complete tolerance certification.

The effective Thevenin resistance is 99.84 kΩ, giving a first pole near 3,392 Hz. The second pole is near 3,386 Hz. Nominal combined attenuation is −0.19 dB at 500 Hz, −0.73 dB at 1 kHz, −2.60 dB at 2 kHz and −10.04 dB at 5 kHz. These filters soften pulse/saw edges, even when their fundamentals are below 1 kHz.

Run python3 scripts/analyze.py to reproduce all values. reports/analog-analysis.json and reports/filter-response.csv are calculated, not measured. simulation/input-channel.cir is an ideal-buffer ngspice starting model; vendor macro-model and transient/fault validation have not been run.

Input loading and open cables

Small-signal input impedance is approximately 1.111 MΩ. The biased resistor network sends approximately 1.50 µA into a source held at 0 V. A 1 kΩ source resistance can therefore create millivolt-scale bias error.

An unconnected input does not read 0 V. With the selected unswitched jack open, its biased node settles near 1.663697 V, which is displayed as approximately +1.663697 V after the nominal conversion. This is an expected P0 limitation, not a detected connected signal. The software must never silently subtract every channel's power-up value, because that would erase legitimate DC voltages.

A later switched jack that shorts the input to ground while unplugged can solve this, but it requires a new exact part, pad/contact map and mechanical qualification. The current PJ-313 5JCJ is not a switched/normalled jack.

Protection is an open engineering gate

±24 V is a test target, not a qualified rating. The 1 MΩ resistor bounds an approximate positive injection current to 24 µA/channel. Ten such channels could inject 240 µA into an unpowered system; a 1 kΩ analog-rail bleed gives an ideal 0.24 V bound under that simplified assumption. Negative injection, individual internal rail paths, ESD, latch-up, diode forward voltages, power sequencing and leakage are not proved by that arithmetic.

Before connecting this prototype to valued equipment, qualify positive/negative powered and unpowered inputs with current-limited fixtures at the assembler or bench-service provider. Never connect mains, speakers/amplifier outputs, unknown industrial voltages or a different ground potential. The instrument is non-isolated and its ground may be bonded to a USB computer.
