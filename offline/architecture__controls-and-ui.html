Physical controls and display behavior

Time and range

TIME is a dedicated 10 kΩ linear ALPS pot for each channel. Its electrical reading maps exponentially from a 2 ms whole-pane window to 8.192 seconds: T = 0.002 × 4096^(code/4095). Endpoint calibration, averaging and a deadband are required to prevent jitter. Keep the physical knob position meaningful; do not turn it into an endless menu encoder.

RANGE is an actual maintained three-position SP3T switch. The selected NKK SS14MDP2 has On-On-On behavior, not a two-position SPDT and not a centre-OFF toggle. Throws select ground, a shared 1.65 V resistor midpoint and analog 3.3 V. The common feeds its own 1 kΩ / 100 nF filter and the RANGE mux. The draft uses logical C/L/M/H terminal labels until its numeric pin map is obtained.

Software decodes low, middle and high bands as ±3, ±5 and ±8 V. Deadbands retain the previous setting; a valid new setting must persist for 20 ms. This avoids intermediate readings during break-before-make travel. Filtering and software debouncing do not qualify the contact material at its actual low current.

HOLD freezes the plots; acquisition continues. LINK makes CH1's TIME control the source for all panes. Turning LINK off immediately restores the ten actual pot positions; it does not overwrite them. Both are press-to-toggle buttons with 20 ms debounce, and small on-screen labels indicate the active mode. LINK is a time-link of views, not phase synchronisation: all channels are still sampled sequentially.

Screen layout

The firmware renderer (firmware/src/scope_render.c, details in firmware/LCD-BACKEND.md) draws ten 160 × 96 logical-pixel panes on the 320 × 480 portrait screen, two columns of five. Numbering is column-major, to preserve the physical banks: left controls 01–05 correspond to the five left-column panes, top to bottom, and right controls 06–10 to the right column, so CH6 is top right. Do not interleave screen labels 01/02 on the first row while the controls use a different order. The simulator follows this assignment, and scripts/validate_extra.py checks the firmware, simulator and panel study against each other.

Each pane has, from top to bottom:

  • a label row: the two-digit channel ID in the channel colour, the vertical scale the plot is drawn at (±3V / ±5V / ±8V), UNCAL while the channel has no installed calibration, and the window duration, right-aligned (2.0ms … 8.19s);
  • a 4-pixel channel colour tag beside a 144 × 66 waveform plot, with the zero line and the newest sample at the right;
  • a status row: the signal token VIEW CLIP or ADC SAT, then LINK and HOLD while those modes are latched;
  • a one-pixel separator.

Text uses a 5 × 7 clean-room font on a 6 × 10 cell grid and is redrawn only when it changes. At the manufacturer's pixel pitch each complete pane is about 24.48 × 14.69 mm. This is a compact visual monitor, not ten large DATA-sized screens. Check the simulator at physical scale and print the panel SVG at 100% before committing the screen size.

TIME mapping on screen

Each pane spans the whole window chosen by its TIME knob (or CH1's under LINK): W = round(0.002 × 4096^(code/4095) s × 10,000 samples/s), from 20 samples (2 ms) to 81,920 samples (8.192 s). The renderer draws it from the smallest min/max history level whose 192 bins cover W, so the existing ten levels reach 8.192 s with no extra memory. Columns merge bins (keeping each column's minimum and maximum, so an extreme anywhere in the window stays visible) or repeat them when there are fewer bins than columns. Each edge of the represented interval is within one coarse bin of the requested window: at most 512 samples, 51.2 ms, at the longest windows. With partial history the retained data sits right-aligned and the not-yet-recorded time stays blank. The duration label and the plot come from the same W, so the label names the interval the plot represents.

RANGE mapping and calibration

The ±3/±5/±8 V ranges are software view scales: the switch does not change the analog path. Each column's raw minimum and maximum codes are converted to volts through that channel's calibration, then ±range maps to the plot height: +range at the top row, 0 V on the zero line, −range 64 rows lower, symmetric about zero. The zero line is electrical zero through the same mapping, not a fixed mid-scale code. With the nominal transfer, a +3 V input reaches the top edge at ±3 V and 3/8 of the positive half-height at ±8 V.

Until a calibration is installed the renderer uses an explicit nominal fallback, the ideal front-end figures in reports/analog-analysis.json (zero code 1858.378, about 8.07 mV/code), and the pane shows UNCAL. No calibration-persistence mechanism exists yet (firmware/tools/calibrate.py only writes a host JSON file), so every pane currently shows UNCAL. Nothing auto-zeroes at startup, and no per-channel calibration data is assumed. scripts/validate_extra.py checks the firmware's nominal constants against the analysis report.

A deadband reading keeps the last valid scale. Before the switch has ever decoded, the plot uses ±8 V and the scale label shows it in the warning colour. The scale label always names the scale the plot is drawn at, so under HOLD it stays with the frozen plot.

Two distinct conditions are reported in the status row. VIEW CLIP means converted volts exceed ±range: the trace is pinned at the edge with a separate warning-coloured marker, and only the view clips. ADC SAT means a raw code of 0 or 4095 in the drawn window, an ADC or input limit; it comes from raw codes, not volts, and takes priority. A clipped ±3 V view never reads as an ADC limit. Neither indicator certifies ±8 V tolerance or proves the absence of an overload between samples. All of this is host-tested against a pixel model; on-screen behaviour on the physical module is still an open G06 item.

Simulator limits

doc/public/prototype/index.html is a synthetic interaction prototype: it generates mathematical waves in the browser. It demonstrates pane layout, clipping, controls, hold and linked time, not measured acquisition fidelity, display refresh performance, hardware noise, multiplexed skew or electrical protection.

No touch feature is used even though the selected purchased module has a touch overlay. A future lower-cost non-touch module can be substituted only after its exact electrical and mechanical interfaces are reviewed.
