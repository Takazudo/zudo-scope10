Acquisition, bandwidth and memory

Three independent mux outputs

U7 selects one of ten buffered signal channels into ADC0. U8 selects one of ten TIME controls into ADC1. U9 selects one of ten RANGE controls into ADC2. They share four address bits and a disable signal, but their analog common outputs are never connected together.

Each mux common output is buffered by its own TLV9064 section, followed by 100 Ω / 1 nF at the corresponding ADC pin. The mux switches already-conditioned unipolar signals, never raw ±12 V inputs. The precise settling time at 3.3 V, charge injection, crosstalk, and ADC acquisition after a large step must be measured.

Intended later high-rate schedule

The Pico SDK ADC documentation specifies 500 ksample/s aggregate and 12-bit conversion, but only about 8.7 effective bits for RP2040. Ten channels do not each get that aggregate rate. Address switching, ADC input switching, settling, firmware overhead and display servicing also consume time.

P0 reserves a 100 µs frame: ten 8 µs signal slots (80 µs), two 8 µs control slots (16 µs), and 4 µs margin. A slot is intended to disable/change/enable the mux, settle, discard one ADC conversion, and retain a second conversion. Each frame reads TIME and RANGE for one advancing control channel, giving a nominal 1 kHz control scan per channel. Every signal would receive one sample/frame, or 10 ksample/s.

This is a timing design target, not an implemented 100 µs real-time engine. The delivered Pico diagnostic intentionally runs much slower and reports raw codes over USB. Develop the high-rate timer/DMA/PIO or tightly bounded acquisition engine only after measuring real settling. Reserve hardware timer timestamps and record overruns instead of silently claiming a rate.

Maximum nominal signal-channel skew is 72 µs between the first and tenth slots. “Ten signals visible” is not simultaneous sampling. LINK TIME changes viewing windows, not sample timing. Phase or transient comparisons require correction for known channel offsets and an explicit common trigger model.

Memory and display

A whole 320 × 480 RGB565 framebuffer is 307,200 bytes. It does not fit in RP2040's 264 kB SRAM. Do not allocate it and hope compression or the linker fixes the problem.

Use streamed strips or tiles; for example a 160 × 16 RGB565 tile is 5,120 bytes, or 10,240 bytes double-buffered. The portable history implementation uses 77,800 bytes for ten channels: ten levels × 192 min/max bins, retaining both extrema through decimation. At 10 ksample/s the coarsest 512-sample bins span about 9.83 seconds, sufficient for the proposed 8.192-second window. Min/max is an envelope representation: do not label those extrema as uniformly resampled raw points.

At 8 MHz, a full RGB565 payload alone takes 307.2 ms; at 24 MHz it takes 102.4 ms. Commands, software and bridge behavior add overhead. P0 starts at 8 MHz. Update only necessary tile regions, reduce redraw rate gracefully, and prioritize acquisition. Never infer 30 FPS full-screen performance from a vendor maximum SPI clock.

Accuracy and clipping

Nominal input-referred ADC code width is about 8.07 mV using 3.3 V / 4096. This is a scale factor, not effective measurement resolution or absolute accuracy. Op-amp/reference error, source impedance, ADC nonlinearity and noise require characterization. UI should show a clear UNCAL indicator until calibration is installed. Display one or two decimals conservatively; do not make an unjustified three-decimal voltmeter.

Distinguish VIEW CLIP (signal exceeds ±3/5/8 pane scale) from ADC SAT (conversion near an ADC rail). A ±8 V display does not certify ±8 V tolerance or prove absence of an overload outside the sampled instant.
